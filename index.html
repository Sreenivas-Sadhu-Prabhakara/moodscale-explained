How it works · 1 of 4

The exact questionnaire — nothing reworded

You answer the public-domain PHQ-9 or GAD-7 with the published wording and the four published response options. Every item is required — no answer is ever silently assumed to be zero.

Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by…

3. Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much

Not at all0 Several days1 More than half the days2 Nearly every day3

Public-domain wording, reproduced verbatim — response values 0–3.

How it works · 2 of 4

The score is pure addition — and it lands on the band

There is no model, no AI, no guesswork. The total is the sum of your item values. Watch nine answers add up, then the marker slide to where that total falls in the published cutoff ranges.

1 2 3 0 1 2 3 0 1 = 13

A PHQ-9 of 13 is shown as Moderate — the published label, cited to Kroenke, Spitzer & Williams (2001). moodscale adds no interpretation of its own.

How it works · 3 of 4

If item 9 isn’t zero, help comes first — calmly

The PHQ-9’s ninth item asks about self-harm. Any answer of 1 or more surfaces a calm helpline panel above the score — unskippable until acknowledged — and a “Need help now?” link stays one tap away in the header and footer.

How it works · 4 of 4

Every check-in is saved — only on your device

Each completed check-in is stored in your browser’s local storage with an ISO timestamp. A per-scale trend chart shades its background using the same published cutoffs as the scorer — one source of truth, no drift.

Raw scores over time, ready to print or export as CSV for your clinician. Trend lines carry measurement error — they are not a measure of treatment response.

The guarantee

Privacy the browser itself enforces

Most apps promise not to send your data. moodscale ships a security policy that makes sending it impossible — the browser blocks every outbound request before it leaves.
